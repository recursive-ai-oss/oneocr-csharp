## [1.1.0] - 2026-11-26

### Added

- **Searchable PDFs** (`RecursiveAI.OneOcr.Pdf`): `image.ToSearchablePdf(...)`, `SearchablePdf.FromImages/FromFiles/FromData` (many images or a multi-frame TIFF into one PDF) and `SearchablePdf.MakeSearchable` (PDF in, PDF out) put the page model's words on the page as an invisible text layer, so selection, search and copy match what the page shows. A JPEG is placed as its own bytes, bilevel pages stay 1-bit, nothing is re-encoded lossily, and rendering is unchanged. `MakeSearchable` leaves pages that already have trusted text exactly as they were, and replaces an invisible layer from an earlier OCR step instead of stacking another on it. PDF/A-2b output is an option. Output is deterministic. `PdfTextLayerWriter` is the low-level writer for one `OcrPage`. Specified in `docs/spec/searchable-pdf.md`.
- **`oneocr pdf`**: the command-line tool makes searchable PDFs. `oneocr pdf <images, folders or one PDF> --out file.pdf [--pdfa] [--title T] [--dpi N] [--keep-ocr-layer] [--degree N]`: several images or a multi-frame TIFF become one PDF, a PDF gets a layer on the pages that need one. One summary line on stdout, notes about the pages on stderr, bad input in one line with a non-zero exit.
- **Running headers and footers on OCR'd pages.** Blocks near the top or bottom edge that repeat across the pages of one call (page numbers count as the same text) get the header or footer role, so local Markdown drops them as it does for text layers. `OcrOptions.MarkHeadersAndFooters` (default on) turns it off.
- **Paragraph splitting.** A block is split at an inter-paragraph gap, a first-line indent, or a short line before a full one; list items never start a split.
- **Hanging-indent labels** (`(a)`, `1.`, clause numbers in a left column) are read together with their clause, not as a separate column.
- **Word and line offsets into text**: `OcrLine.GetWordSpans()`, `OcrPage.GetWordSpans()` and `GetLineSpans()` return `OcrTextSpan` values for the text the page joins.
- **Non-square pixels** (standard-mode fax, 200 by 100 dots per inch). The reader now takes the resolution down the page as well as across it (PNG `pHYs`, JFIF density, TIFF `XResolution` and `YResolution`, or the new `OcrOptions.VerticalDpi`). A page whose two resolutions differ by more than 5% is stretched to square pixels before it is read, a stretched 1-bit page is lightly smoothed, and every box and quad is mapped back, so coordinates stay in the pixels of the image you passed in. `OcrPage.VerticalUnitsPerInch` and the page JSON member `unitsPerInchY` (written only when it differs from `unitsPerInch`) carry the second resolution, and searchable PDFs use both, so a fax page has the shape of the paper. On generated standard-mode fax pages the share of amounts misread fell from 40–50% to 11–16%. New notes `OCR112` (stretched) and `OCR113` (declared resolutions not used). Pages with square pixels take no extra step.
- **`MakeSearchable` marks unreliable visible text with an empty `/ActualText`.** Visible text with a garbled or wrong character map used to stay in the file beside our layer, so extraction could show both. The shows that paint it are now wrapped in marked content (`/Span <</ActualText ()>> BDC … EMC`), so extraction and copy return the recognized words. No other byte of the page changes and rendering is identical.
- **A financial-document benchmark** in `bench/`: generators for statements (with running balances), invoices and receipts, degraded as scans, standard- and fine-mode fax, phone photos and tables; a slice of public US Treasury reports; and scorer v3.2 with *amount exact match* (every money value, date and account-like number read exactly) and statement running-balance reconciliation. The sets are fetched or generated by script and are not in the repository.

### Changed

- **Behaviour changes to know about.** Output of 1.0 can differ from 1.1 in these ways, all on purpose (amount forms are the next item):
  - *Reading order on multi-column pages* changes (see the item on columns below) where a short column or a title sits (next items); text and boxes of the words themselves do not change.
  - *Headers and footers are marked on OCR'd pages* (`OcrOptions.MarkHeadersAndFooters`, default on), so `ToMarkdown()` now drops running headers, footers and page numbers of scanned pages as it already did for text layers. Set the option to false for the old output.
  - *Blocks are split into paragraphs* at paragraph gaps, first-line indents and short lines before full ones, so a page can have more blocks than before.
  - *Pages with non-square pixels are stretched before reading* (fax): their text, boxes and page height differ from 1.0, which read them squashed. Pages with square pixels are unaffected.
  - *More words are found* on scans and phone photos (line ends, lone digits, small numbers); a page that lost a character at the end of a line in 1.0 now has it.
- **Amounts have one canonical form (behaviour change).** Money values always carry at least two decimals: `126` becomes `126.00` and `36.5` becomes `36.50` in extracted fields, in `GroundedField.NormalizedValue` and in local-versus-LLM comparisons. Quantities and percentages keep the shortest exact decimal (`2` stays `2`, `7.50` is `7.5`). Code that compared these strings to whole-number text should compare decimal values.
- **Columns of text next to a shorter column, or under a title.** A page of two or three columns of running text was read in slices when a title stood over the first column only, or when the first column went on below the end of the second (the short column landed in the middle of the long one). Columns of paragraphs are now read column by column, and a one- or two-line title over them is read first, on upright and tilted pages alike. Cards, grids and screens keep their reading.
- `oneocr ocr` reads files from their path, so the resolution a file declares reaches the page (it used to read the pixels only, and estimate the resolution from the text size).
- Indented `oneocr.page` JSON keeps bounds, quads and number lists on one line. The compact form is unchanged byte for byte.
- `RecursiveAI.OneOcr.Pdf` builds on `RecursiveAI.OnePdf` 1.25.0 (was 1.10.1).

### Fixed

- **Due date versus delivery date.** On an invoice that prints only a due date, the local model could tag it as the delivery date (`BT-72`). The label printed to the left of the value now decides between `BT-9` and `BT-72`.
- **Small numbers on photographed pages are no longer taken for noise.** On a phone photo of a statement the amounts and dates can be printed small, and the noise filter dropped every unsure word of a small line, so amounts came back as nothing at all. A word of digits and number punctuation (at least two digits) is now kept in a small line when the page has at least three of them. On generated phone pages the amount exact match rises by 0.2 to 0.5 points on top of the line-end change, and every other development set is unchanged. Specified in `docs/spec/oneocr-page-v1.md` (Noise filter).
- **Line ends on noisy scans.** A photocopied or JPEG-noisy scan is noisy enough to take the line finder's camera-photo path, which never followed a line's ink past the end of the detector's box. The box often stops a letter short, so the last digit of a number, a trailing minus sign or the first letter of a description was lost (`160.97-` read `160.97`, `447957` read `44795`). Ends are now followed on these pages as on clean ones, and a line that leans on its own (read along its axis) is cut from a strip that reaches past the detected component, so its ends can move too. On generated scans of statements, invoices and receipts at 150 to 300 dpi and 0.5 to 8 degrees of skew, the share of amounts read wrongly fell from 3.5% to 1.9% (character error 0.58% to 0.49%, word error 2.15% to 1.62%), and generated phone pages gain as well. Specified in `docs/spec/detector-v1.md` (Line ends).
- **Lone digits in table rows are read.** A quantity, a line number or a one-digit cell is too small for the detector's text map to cross its page threshold, so these digits were missing from the page (about half of the quantity cells on degraded financial scans, fax and phone pages). The detector's post-processing now also finds weak isolated marks in the rows of ordinary text and keeps what is read from them only when it is a run of at most four digits with confidence of 0.9 or more. Specified in `docs/spec/detector-v1.md` (Lone digits).
- **The minus sign of an amount is read.** On scans and phone captures the sign of `-1,370.35` or `4,981.95-` is a thin stroke a few pixels from its digits that lay just outside the crop the recognizer reads, so the amount came back unsigned. A line that starts or ends with a number is now checked for such a stroke past the end of its ink; when there is one, the line is read a second time with the stroke inside the crop, and the second reading replaces the first only if it differs by that one sign, so a sign is added only when the recognizer reads it and nothing else can change. On generated pages of statements, invoices and receipts (new seeds), lost minus signs fell from 25 to 1 of 255 on scans, from 70 to 58 of 164 on phone captures (the rest are blurred beyond reading) and from 19 to 11 of 295 on tables; on the development pages the amount exact match rises by 1.0 point on scans (94.5 to 95.6), 0.8 on tables and 0.2 on phone and fax pages, and reconciled statement rows by 3.4 and 5.5 points on scans and tables. Parentheses are not searched for. A few amounts in several thousand gain a sign they should not have (dust beside a number). Other test sets are unchanged except for two pages that gain a sign or dash printed in the text. Specified in `docs/spec/detector-v1.md` (Signs beside a number).


## [1.0.0] - 2026-10-31

First release.

### Added

- **Local OCR** (`RecursiveAI.OneOcr`): a pure-managed reader for scans, born-digital renders, screenshots and phone photos of flat documents, with no dependency beyond the base class library and `RecursiveAI.OneImage`. Latin (including the EU languages, Turkish and Vietnamese), Greek and Cyrillic in one recognizer. Page orientation (0/90/180/270) and deskew are on by default.
- **Deterministic output.** Text, boxes and confidences are bit-identical on AVX-512, AVX2, 128-bit vectors (SSE and NEON) and the scalar path, and at every degree of parallelism. The numeric rules are specified in `docs/spec/numerics.md`.
- **The page model** (`OcrPage`, blocks, lines, words, characters, tables, key-value pairs, a `Source` and a calibrated `Confidence` on every element) and its versioned `oneocr.page` JSON, which shares member names with OnePDF's `onepdf.extraction`.
- **Calibrated confidence.** A word's confidence is the probability that the word is exactly right, calibrated per script on held-out data.
- **Local Markdown** from the page model: headings, lists, pipe tables, with headers and footers dropped.
- **Document extraction**: `ExtractInvoice()`, `ExtractReceipt()` and `Extract<T>()` over a small local model that labels words on the page, so a value always carries the words and boxes it was read from. Invoice fields carry their EN 16931 business-term ids. The model ships in `RecursiveAI.OneOcr.Models.Documents`.
- **LLM vision** (`RecursiveAI.OneOcr.Llm`): Anthropic, OpenAI, OpenAI-compatible servers, Gemini and Azure OpenAI over a plain `HttpClient`. Every returned value is checked against the words on the page and comes back `Grounded`, `GroundedNormalized`, `Derived` or `Ungrounded`. Cost estimates and caps, routing between local models and the LLM, an `Offline` switch, a redaction hook and no-retention flags. Nothing leaves the machine unless you configure a provider.
- **Fine-tuning** (`RecursiveAI.OneOcr.Training`): recognition and extraction fine-tuning on your own documents, on the CPU, deterministic and resumable, with a review queue for lines the reader and the LLM disagree on.
- **PDF pages** (`RecursiveAI.OneOcr.Pdf`): text-layer words where a page has them, local OCR for scanned regions and image-only pages, one page model.
- **The `oneocr` command-line tool** (`RecursiveAI.OneOcr.Tool`): `ocr`, `train`, `eval` and `review`.
- **The model container**, a versioned binary format for weights, specified in `docs/spec/model-container-v1.md`; the bundled recognizer, page detector and language models are embedded in `RecursiveAI.OneOcr.Models` and checked against their SHA-256 pins on every load.
- Parsers for model files, images, page JSON, PDFs, review files and LLM responses are bounds-checked, run under limits checked before anything large is allocated, and have fuzz targets. NativeAOT and trimming are supported.

### Known limits

- Not supported: CJK, Arabic and Indic scripts, handwriting, curved-page dewarping and scene text.
- The README's benchmark tables list every category where a competing engine reads better than OneOcr.
- A NativeAOT build for x64 needs an instruction-set level of at least `x86-64-v3`; the package sets it by default. Below it the reader runs its exact scalar path, about a hundred times slower, with the same text.
